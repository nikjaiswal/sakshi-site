# PRODUCT.md

Product context for design tooling such as Impeccable. Keep this short and
factual; it is read by agents before they change the interface.

## What this is

The public website of SAKSHI (Structured Accountability through Knowledge,
Scrutiny, and Health Integrity), a community-operated investigative platform
for accountability in clinical AI deployment. SAKSHI is a project of MIT
Critical Data, the Laboratory for Computational Physiology at MIT.

The site explains the proposal, publishes the investigative protocol, hosts
a fillable checklist for auditors, and will publish company profiles once
the first regional hubs operate. It is a public evidentiary record, and the
repository's edit history is part of that record.

## Status

A proposal under development. No profiles have been published. The
framework's claims have not yet been tested. The site states this plainly
on every page, and design work must not soften or hide it.

## Who uses it

- Prospective community auditors: medical students, clinicians, patient
  advocates, data scientists, public health practitioners, mostly in low-
  and middle-income countries, often on phones and slow connections.
- Partners: investigative journalists, health law scholars, medical
  specialty societies, patient advocacy organizations, regulators and
  procurement officers evaluating whether to engage.
- Companies that may be profiled, reading the governance and appeals
  process.
- Readers of the paper looking for the living version of the framework.

## What they need

- To understand quickly what SAKSHI is, what it does and does not do, and
  that it is not yet operating.
- To read the protocol and governance in full, without marketing language.
- To use the Minimum Viable Template checklist on any device and keep
  their entries in their own browser.
- To find the publications and the source of every claim.

## Constraints

- Tone is a methods document, not a product launch: plain declarative
  sentences, no superlatives, no em dashes.
- Never name a real company as a subject of investigation. The case study
  is a hypothetical composite and is labeled as such.
- No analytics, no tracking, no third-party scripts. The only external
  resource is the Inter typeface from Google Fonts.
- Static site, plain HTML and CSS, minimal vanilla JavaScript. Works
  without JavaScript.
- Root-relative links; built with SITE_BASE for the GitHub Pages subpath.

  MIT Critical Data (MIT License).
