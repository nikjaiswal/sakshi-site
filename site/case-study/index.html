Case Study

AfyaScan AI in Uganda

A walk through all four investigative domains on a composite deployment.

This is a hypothetical composite case. AfyaScan AI does not exist. The scenario is drawn from patterns observed across multiple real companies deploying health AI in sub-Saharan Africa, and it depicts no real company, product, or individual. Its purpose is to show how the four-domain protocol operates in practice.

The scenario

AfyaScan AI is a venture-backed startup that has developed a vision-language diagnostic support model for chest X-ray interpretation and deployed it across 14 district hospitals in Uganda. ("Afya" is Swahili for "health.")

The company's website claims it has "impacted 2.3 million lives across East Africa" and that its algorithm "matches radiologist-level accuracy in TB detection." It holds an FDA 510(k) clearance obtained in the United States and uses that clearance in marketing materials across all deployment contexts. The Ministry of Health has signed a memorandum of understanding with AfyaScan, and the company's regional director has presented at several African health summits.

AfyaScan's work represents a real investment in diagnostic access in under-resourced settings, and that recognition does not rule out careful, fact-based scrutiny. SAKSHI's posture toward the company is investigative, not prosecutorial.

A real profile would name the company under review as a matter of course. SAKSHI profiles are public and attributable by design, unlike blind peer review, so the identity of this composite is deliberately not held back from the reader. What stands in for a real company here is a composite drawn from public patterns rather than a genuine finding about anyone.

The auditor team

A SAKSHI community auditor team based in Kampala applies the protocol. In the composite, the team consists of two medical students, a clinical officer from a national referral hospital, a patient advocate from a TB survivors' organization, and a data science trainee.

Composite scenario, domain 1

Clinical evidence audit

The team searches ClinicalTrials.gov and the Pan-African Clinical Trials Registry for registered trials involving AfyaScan in Uganda or East Africa, and records what it does not find.

For each peer-reviewed publication the company cites, the team checks whether the study actually supports the claim attached to it. The "radiologist-level accuracy" claim draws specific questions: accuracy against which radiologists, in which population, against what reference standard, and whether the validation data reflected Ugandan patients, including HIV-TB coinfection, pediatric cases, and patients with prior TB scarring.

The team also checks the FDA clearance dataset for how representative its population was. The absence of local validation is itself recorded as a finding.

Composite scenario, domain 2

Regulatory pathway analysis

Uganda's National Drug Authority treats software as a medical device but has no AI-specific guidance, risk classification, or post-market surveillance for algorithmic systems. The team documents that gap.

It then traces how AfyaScan actually entered the market, whether through the memorandum of understanding, a procurement process, or a partnership with an international health organization, and whether anyone ran an independent clinical evaluation before deployment.

The question that anchors this domain is whether AfyaScan's FDA clearance is being used to imply validation that does not reach the Ugandan context.

Composite scenario, domain 3

Financial disclosure map

The team identifies AfyaScan's investors, its board, and any advisory ties to government officials or public health bodies in Uganda.

It examines performance contracts tied to scan volume for misaligned incentives, in particular whether the financial structure rewards throughput at the expense of diagnostic accuracy.

If the memorandum of understanding involves payment or in-kind transfers, the team weighs whether that creates the kind of government entanglement that, as one team member from Uganda put it during the platform's development, means "attacking the company is a direct attack on the government." Where that dynamic is present, it is documented.

Composite scenario, domain 4

Patient experience reporting

The team interviews patients at deployment sites to assess whether they know an AI system is interpreting their X-rays, whether they were offered a choice, and whether they have any path to seek recourse if they believe a diagnosis was missed.

The workflow is documented. Does a clinical officer review every AI-generated result, or are some acted on directly? What happens when the AI flags an abnormality the clinical officer disagrees with, or the reverse?

What the profile does

The completed profile for AfyaScan would be published on this platform, structured for comparison with profiles of similar companies in other countries, and updated as the deployment evolves.

If AfyaScan's product is genuinely effective, a rigorous profile strengthens its credibility with procurement officers, health ministries, and international funders. If it is not, the profile provides the evidentiary basis for corrective action before patients are harmed.

"In Uganda, when you talk about anything tech and you're selling people hope, people get excited. They don't know how this thing works. They don't know the implications. If we go into this research and inform people, then better decisions are made."

A member of the SAKSHI team from Uganda, during the platform's development.
