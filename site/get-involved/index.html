Get Involved

Join the auditor network

SAKSHI is run by trained community auditors in the places where health AI is deployed. The network is being formed now.

Who auditors are

Auditor teams are multi-stakeholder by construction. They bring together:

  • medical students and clinical trainees;
  • clinicians, including clinical officers and nurses;
  • patient advocates and members of civil society organizations;
  • data scientists and biomedical engineers;
  • public health practitioners.

The four domains of the protocol follow from what such a team can and should judge. Clinical evidence and technical performance draw on clinical and data-science expertise. Regulatory pathway and financial disclosure draw on the institutional and political knowledge that comes from working inside the health system in question. Patient experience draws on standing as a member of the affected community, which no outside expert can substitute for.

Local auditors are investigators, not an advisory panel. They apply the protocol, make the evidentiary judgments, and author the findings.

Regional hubs

SAKSHI runs through regional community hubs rather than a centralized team issuing judgments from Cambridge, Massachusetts. The hubs draw on the MIT Critical Data consortium's presence in over 40 countries, a network built through more than a hundred datathons over the past decade.

Each hub trains local auditors to apply the protocol in their own context, keeps a current registry of certified auditors, and calibrates periodically against profiles produced at other hubs. Oversight runs upward, from the people living with the consequences of a deployment toward the institutions that made the decisions, through a shared protocol that makes local findings legible elsewhere.

The training pipeline

Certification runs in three phases: a self-directed online module, a live workshop on a standardized reference case, and a supervised field contribution under a senior auditor. Certification lapses on a fixed cycle and is renewed against an updated reference case. Every auditor completes a conflict of interest disclosure before working on a profile. The full process is described under Governance.

Scrutinizing powerful companies creates professional and legal exposure. Profiles are published under the consortium's institutional name, legal counsel is retained centrally, and disputes run through a documented appeals process rather than through pressure on individual auditors. See how legal risk is distributed.

Partnerships

A published profile is where the work starts. The pathway from observation to change runs through several channels at once, and SAKSHI is seeking partners in each of them:

  • Investigative journalists, who pick up findings and amplify them.
  • Health law scholars, who help keep published findings within the bounds of evidence-based reporting and draw on them in legal argument.
  • Medical specialty societies, whose members encounter these systems in practice and can judge clinical claims.
  • Patient advocacy organizations, who draw on profiles to demand transparency, meaningful informed consent, and renegotiation of agreements with health AI companies.
  • Regulators and procurement bodies, who cite profiles in procurement decisions and draw on documented patterns when drafting future guidance.
